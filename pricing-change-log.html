What Counts as a Pricing Change?

PRICE

Price change

A published subscription price moves from one amount to another.

PLAN

Plan change

A provider changes what a plan includes, removes a plan or introduces a new tier.

LIMIT

Limit change

A seat, contact, automation, usage or feature limit changes the economics of a plan.

ADD-ON

Add-on change

An additional feature or service changes price or becomes separately chargeable.

How SaaSRealCost Records a Change

Provider: The affected SaaS product.

Observed date: When SaaSRealCost verified the information.

Effective date: When the provider says the change takes effect, when available.

Previous value: The documented price, limit or plan condition before the change.

New value: The documented price, limit or plan condition after the change.

Buyer scenario: Which type of customer is affected and how.

Real-cost impact: The calculated effect for a defined scenario where enough information is available.

Source: Preferably the provider's own pricing page, documentation or announcement.

Score review: Whether the change requires a review of the provider's Real Cost Score.

Why a Pricing History Matters

A current pricing page shows what a buyer may pay today. A change log adds another layer: how pricing, limits and plan structure evolve over time. That matters because a low entry price can become less relevant if a provider later changes tiers, limits, required add-ons or billing terms.

SaaSRealCost therefore treats pricing history as evidence that can feed into provider research, Real Cost Scores and scenario calculations — rather than as a collection of unsupported historical claims.
