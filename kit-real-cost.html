Kit's $0 price changes the usual email marketing calculation

Kit currently offers a free plan for creators with up to 10,000 subscribers. The free plan includes unlimited forms and landing pages, unlimited email broadcasts, audience tagging and segmentation, and digital products and subscriptions.

That makes Kit unusual compared with email platforms where the cost can increase relatively early as a list grows.

What actually makes Kit more expensive?

1. Your subscriber count

Kit's paid pricing is subscriber-based. As your active subscriber count moves into a higher pricing tier, your monthly cost can increase.

Kit states that it charges based on confirmed active subscribers rather than simply charging for the number of emails you send.

2. The features you need

A creator with a small newsletter may be perfectly comfortable with the free plan. Someone building automated funnels may need a paid plan because visual automations and automated sequences are part of the paid offering.

3. List growth

Growth is good—but growth can also change your software costs. If you cross a subscriber threshold, Kit can automatically move the account to the next pricing tier.

4. Unnecessary subscribers

A large inactive list can increase your software bill without giving you additional value. Regularly cleaning inactive subscribers can therefore become part of your real email marketing cost strategy.

Kit cost as your audience grows

Scaling business

At larger subscriber counts, the monthly software cost becomes a more important part of the economics of your newsletter or email business.

One thing many buyers overlook: list hygiene

Your subscriber count affects your cost on paid Kit plans. That makes inactive subscribers more than a marketing problem—they can also become a cost problem.

Kit itself recommends cleaning your list and explains that reducing your confirmed subscriber count can allow you to move back into a lower pricing tier.

In other words: growing your audience is important, but managing the quality of that audience matters too.
