HOSTING FOR SMALL BUSINESS

Best web hosting for a small business website.

A business site has different needs than a personal blog: professional email, uptime you can rely on, and room to grow without a painful migration later.

What a small business site actually needs

  • Storage: Most small business sites (5-20 pages, some images) run comfortably on 10-50GB. You rarely need "unlimited" storage unless you're hosting large media libraries.
  • Number of websites: If you only run one site now, don't overpay for a multi-site plan—check if upgrading later is simple first.
  • Professional email: A business needs a proper @yourdomain.com inbox, not a free Gmail address. Check how many mailboxes are included before paying extra.
  • Speed: Slow load times cost customers. Look for SSD storage and a content delivery network (CDN) at minimum.
  • Security & SSL: A free SSL certificate should be standard, not an add-on. Check what additional security monitoring costs.
  • Backups: Daily automatic backups matter more for a business site than a personal one—downtime costs money.

The renewal trap

Many hosts advertise a low first-year price that jumps significantly at renewal. For a business, budget based on the renewal price, not the introductory offer—that's the number you'll actually pay long-term.

When to upgrade

If your site is a core part of how customers find or contact your business, it's usually worth paying for managed or higher-tier hosting rather than the cheapest shared plan. The cost difference is often small compared to lost business from downtime.

Frequently asked

Do I need business hosting, or is regular hosting fine?

Regular shared hosting works for many small businesses. "Business" plans usually just add more storage, mailboxes and priority support—useful, but not always essential at the start.

How much should I budget per month?

Look at the renewal price, not the intro offer. Use our hosting cost calculator to see the real total over several years.

Is free hosting ever a good idea for a business?

Generally no—free hosting often lacks a custom domain, professional email, reliable uptime and support, all of which matter for a business's credibility.
