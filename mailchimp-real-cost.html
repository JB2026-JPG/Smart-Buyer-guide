What makes Mailchimp more expensive than the starting number suggests?

  • Your contact count matters. Growing the audience moves you into higher pricing tiers.
  • Inactive contacts can matter. Mailchimp's pricing model counts contacts beyond simply the people who currently receive your campaigns, so list hygiene can affect cost.
  • Email volume matters too. Each plan has sending limits and overage rules.
  • The free plan is small. The current Free plan is limited to 250 contacts, making the move to paid pricing relevant much earlier than it once was.
  • SMS and other services can be separate. Mailchimp identifies SMS as an add-on in eligible markets rather than something automatically included in every plan.

Who should consider Mailchimp?

Mailchimp can make sense when you want a mature email-marketing platform with broad integrations, automation and marketing tools in one ecosystem. It is less attractive if your main priority is keeping the long-term cost of a growing subscriber list as low and predictable as possible.

Before you commit

  • Check the price at your expected contact count, not only the entry price.
  • Decide how often you will clean or archive inactive contacts.
  • Check the monthly sending allowance against your campaign frequency.
  • Separate core email cost from optional SMS or other paid services.
  • Compare the expected cost at 1,000, 5,000 and 10,000 contacts before choosing a platform.
