CHEAP HOSTING GUIDE

Cheap web hosting: what to check before you buy.

Cheap doesn't always mean cheap. A low sticker price can hide costs that show up later—here's what to verify first.

The introductory price isn't the real price

Budget hosts often advertise a heavily discounted first term. Always find the renewal price—usually listed in smaller text or in the terms—before you commit.

Check what's actually included

  • Domain renewal: A "free domain" is often only free for year one. Check the renewal cost.
  • Email: Some cheap plans don't include email hosting at all, or limit you to one mailbox.
  • Backups: Automatic backups are sometimes a paid add-on on budget plans.
  • SSL certificate: Should be free—if it's not, that's a sign to look elsewhere.
  • Site migration: Moving to a cheap host is often free; moving away later may not be.

Hidden add-ons to watch for

Some budget providers use upsells during checkout—security add-ons, extra backups, priority support—that can significantly increase the advertised price. Read the checkout page carefully before confirming payment.

When cheap hosting makes sense

For a personal blog, portfolio, or low-traffic hobby site, a budget host is often perfectly fine. For a business site where downtime costs money, the cheapest option is rarely the best value.

Frequently asked

Is the cheapest host always the worst choice?

Not necessarily—some budget hosts offer solid value. The key is checking renewal pricing and included features rather than judging by the intro price alone.

Why do prices increase so much after the first term?

Hosts use low intro pricing to attract new customers, similar to a subscription free trial. It's a standard industry practice—the key is knowing the real number in advance.
