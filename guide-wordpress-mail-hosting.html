Why WordPress Email Keeps Failing on Google Compute Engine (And the Easier Fix)

Smart Buyer Guide · Hosting guides

If you've searched for a way to get outbound email working on a WordPress site hosted on Google Compute Engine, you already know the frustration: no built-in mail server, SMTP ports blocked by default, and a maze of DNS records (SPF, DKIM, DMARC) you have to configure by hand just to stop your emails from landing in spam.

This isn't a WordPress problem. It's a self-managed infrastructure problem. Google Compute Engine gives you a raw virtual machine — powerful, but it assumes you're comfortable acting as your own systems administrator. Email delivery is one of the first things that exposes that gap.

Why this keeps happening

No mail service included. Compute Engine is infrastructure-as-a-service — you get compute and storage, not a configured mail transfer agent.
Port 25 is blocked by default on most cloud providers, including Google Cloud, to prevent spam abuse. You typically need to request an exception or route through a third-party relay (like SendGrid or Mailgun) just to send mail.
DNS authentication is manual. SPF, DKIM, and DMARC records all need to be added correctly, or your emails get flagged as spam regardless of the relay you use.
You're responsible for everything else too — security patches, backups, SSL renewal, and uptime, all of which come free on managed hosting.

The simpler path: managed WordPress hosting

If your goal is a working website with reliable email — not a side project in cloud server administration — managed WordPress hosting removes this entire category of problem. Email accounts, SSL, backups, and security are configured for you from the start, so there's no relay to set up and no DNS records to debug at 11pm.
