What is hosting, actually?

Hosting is the service that stores your website's files and makes them accessible on the internet. Without it, your site simply doesn't exist online—think of it as renting space for your website to live.

Do you need a domain separately?

Usually yes, though many hosts sell domains alongside hosting or include one free for the first year. A domain (yourname.com) and hosting (where the site lives) are technically two separate purchases, even when bundled together.

Should you use WordPress?

For most beginners, yes. WordPress is free, widely supported, and most hosts offer one-click installation. It has a learning curve, but far less than building a site from scratch.

What about SSL?

SSL (the padlock icon in your browser) should be free and automatic with any host you choose in 2026. If a host charges extra for basic SSL, that's a red flag.

Common beginner mistakes

  • Buying way more storage or bandwidth than a new site needs
  • Not checking the renewal price before signing up
  • Skipping backups because "it won't happen to me"
  • Choosing a host with no live chat or beginner-friendly support

Frequently asked

Can I switch hosts later if I choose wrong?

Yes, though it takes some effort. Most hosts offer migration help, and WordPress sites are generally easier to move than custom-built ones.

Do I need technical skills to manage hosting?

Not for basic use. Most control panels are point-and-click. Technical skill becomes more useful as your site grows more complex.
