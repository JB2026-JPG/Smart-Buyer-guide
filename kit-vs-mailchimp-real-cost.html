Kit vs Mailchimp Real Cost: Which Is Cheaper as You Grow?

The cheaper email marketing platform isn't always the one with the lower starting price.

Kit and Mailchimp use different pricing structures, different audience limits and different approaches to features. The better question is: which one makes more financial sense for your situation?

The biggest difference: how the pricing works

Kit currently offers a Free plan for up to 10,000 subscribers. Its Creator plan is currently shown at $33 per month for 1,000 subscribers, while Pro is shown at $66 per month for 1,000 subscribers. [oai_citation:1‡Kit](https://kit.com/pricing?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

Mailchimp uses a different structure. Its current marketing plans include Free, Essentials, Standard and Premium, with pricing determined by the plan and contact tier. Mailchimp also places limits on monthly sends depending on the plan. [oai_citation:2‡Mailchimp](https://mailchimp.com/pricing/marketing/?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

That difference matters because two businesses with the same number of people on their email list can end up with very different software costs.

Scenario 1: You're just starting

If you are starting a newsletter and have only a small audience, the headline pricing difference can be significant.

Kit's current Free plan supports up to 10,000 subscribers, although some advanced features require a paid plan. [oai_citation:3‡Kit](https://kit.com/pricing?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

Mailchimp's current Free plan supports up to 250 contacts and 500 sends per month. If you exceed the contact limit, Mailchimp says sending can be paused until you upgrade or reduce your contact total. [oai_citation:4‡Mailchimp](https://mailchimp.com/help/about-mailchimp-pricing-plans/?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

Winner for a creator who wants to build a list without paying?

Kit has a significant advantage on the current free-plan limits.

But that doesn't automatically mean Kit is the best platform for every business. Your required features matter.

Scenario 2: Your list reaches 1,000 people

This is where the comparison becomes more interesting.

Kit currently displays its Creator plan at $33 per month for 1,000 subscribers and Pro at $66 per month. [oai_citation:5‡Kit](https://kit.com/pricing?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

Mailchimp's current pricing also changes according to the contact tier and plan you choose. Its displayed entry pricing includes Essentials at $13/month and Standard at $20/month, but the exact amount depends on the selected contact count and plan. [oai_citation:6‡Mailchimp](https://mailchimp.com/pricing/marketing/?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

So a simple “Kit costs more” conclusion would be misleading.

The real comparison is: What features do you get for the money, and which features do you actually need?

Scenario 3: Your audience keeps growing

This is where the real cost becomes more important than the introductory price.

With Kit

Kit's Free plan can support up to 10,000 subscribers, but paid plans become relevant when you need features such as visual automations, unlimited email sequences, A/B testing and other advanced functionality. [oai_citation:7‡Kit](https://kit.com/pricing?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

With Mailchimp

Mailchimp's pricing is tied to contact tiers and plan level. The company also defines contacts broadly across audiences, and the selected contact limit determines the pricing tier. [oai_citation:8‡Mailchimp](https://mailchimp.com/help/mailchimp-pricing-tiers/?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

The important question

Don't ask only “Which is cheaper today?” Ask “Which pricing model will still make sense when my business is bigger?”

Where Mailchimp can make more sense

Kit should not automatically win this comparison.

Mailchimp may make more sense if you are primarily running a broader marketing operation and need features designed around marketing campaigns, automation, integrations and larger business workflows.

Mailchimp currently promotes integrations with hundreds of tools and offers different marketing plans designed for growing businesses. [oai_citation:9‡Mailchimp](https://mailchimp.com/en/?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

  • You need a broad marketing platform
  • You work with multiple marketing channels
  • You need business-oriented marketing features
  • You prefer a traditional marketing platform
  • You need features available in Mailchimp's higher plans

Where Kit can make more sense

Kit is particularly interesting for creators, newsletter businesses and people who want to build an audience around content or expertise.

Kit currently emphasizes creators and offers a Free plan up to 10,000 subscribers. Its paid plans add visual automations, sequences, analytics, subscriber insights and other creator-focused features. [oai_citation:10‡Kit](https://kit.com/pricing?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

  • You are building a newsletter
  • You are a creator or content business
  • You want a large free starting allowance
  • You want creator-focused features
  • You plan to sell digital products or subscriptions

Don't ignore the cost of duplicate contacts

There is another difference worth understanding.

Kit states that its subscriber pricing is based on unique active subscribers. Kit specifically argues that Mailchimp can charge for the same person when that subscriber exists across multiple lists. [oai_citation:11‡Kit](https://kit.com/compare/mailchimp?lmref=PqcvGA&utm_source=chatgpt.com)

This is one reason why comparing only the advertised monthly prices can give you the wrong answer.

Your actual list structure matters.

So which one should you choose?

If you are primarily building a creator newsletter and want a large free starting allowance, Kit deserves serious consideration.

If you are running a broader marketing operation and need Mailchimp's marketing ecosystem, Mailchimp may be the better fit.

The cheapest option today is not necessarily the cheapest option for the business you are building.

Before switching email platforms

  • How many active subscribers do you have?
  • How many emails do you send each month?
  • Do you need automated sequences?
  • Do you need advanced marketing automation?
  • How many audiences or lists do you maintain?
  • Will your costs change significantly as your audience grows?
  • How difficult will migration be?

Kit provides migration guidance for people moving from Mailchimp, including importing contacts and rebuilding forms, templates and automations. [oai_citation:12‡Kit Help Center](https://help.kit.com/en/articles/2502565-migrating-from-mailchimp-to-kit?utm_source=chatgpt.com)
